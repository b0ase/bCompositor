Why bCompositor

Your editor knows how every image was made. Now it can prove it.

A Bitcoin button that inscribes a JPEG isn't a reason to switch editors. These are things only the editor can do, because only the editor saw the work happen.

Built

Git-style history

Each save becomes a version. Layers that didn't change are stored once, so a 40-layer composite with one edit costs one layer, not a new copy. Restore any version with one undoable step.

Next

Proof of authorship

A signed, timestamped record of the sources, layers and edits behind an image — including whether any AI fill was used. Like Content Credentials, without depending on Adobe’s servers.

Next

Proof you had it first

Each saved version timestamped on Bitcoin (BSV), so if your work is copied you can show your layered original existed earlier. Only hashes go on-chain; your images stay yours.

Coming next

Share a whole project with one link.

A bcomp: link works like a magnet link for a project’s history. Open it to view every version, fork it to start your own branch, or — if the owner lets you — keep editing it together.

Edits from two people merge layer by layer: change the sky while they change the face, and both land. Access can be revoked at any time, and every change is signed by the person who made it.

  • xtThe project’s identity: the Bitcoin transaction that started its history.
  • wsWhere the layer files can be fetched. Every file is checked against its hash, so the host doesn’t need to be trusted.
  • #kThe read key. It sits after the #, so it never reaches a server.
  • #wOptional edit access, which the owner can revoke.
A real editor first

Everything you’d use Photoshop for, on your Mac.

bCompositor is built on Compositor, the open-source Mac image editor, and keeps pace with it. You get the full editor before any of the Bitcoin features.

  • Layers and folders with blend modes, opacity and drag-and-drop nesting
  • Masks: layer, clipping and folder masks you can paint, blur and feather
  • Adjustment layers: Levels, Curves, Hue/Saturation, Exposure, Gradient Map, Grain
  • Non-destructive transforms, free distort and snapping with guides
  • Selections: marquee, lasso, Magic Wand, Select Subject
  • Content-Aware Fill, Spot Healing and Clone Stamp
  • Live layer effects: stroke, drop shadow, inner shadow, outer glow
  • Editable type and shapes that stay sharp when scaled
  • Photoshop PSD import, plus JPEG, PNG, HEIC, TIFF and camera raw
  • Photoshop shortcuts throughout, remappable

Compositor is made by Wonder Assembly and released under the MIT licence. bCompositor is an independent edition and opens Compositor projects.

Questions

Good to know

What do I need to run it?

A Mac on macOS 26.5 or later. Download the disk image, drag bCompositor to Applications, and it updates itself from then on. It sits happily alongside Compositor and opens Compositor projects.

Is it free?

Yes. The editor and version history are free and open source. Recording versions on Bitcoin will cost a fraction of a cent per save in network fees, and it’s optional.

Do I need a Bitcoin wallet?

No. Editing and version history work without one. For the on-chain features you’ll be able to connect Metanet Desktop or HandCash, and HandCash users get a built-in wallet for anything HandCash can’t hold.

Do my images go on the blockchain?

No — only small fingerprints (hashes) and signatures. Your layers stay on your Mac, and in storage you choose when you share a project. Putting a version fully on-chain will be an option, not the default.

Which Bitcoin?

BSV, where recording data costs a tiny fraction of what it does elsewhere. That is what makes a record of every save practical.
